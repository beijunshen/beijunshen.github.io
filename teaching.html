Teaching

Mechanics describes how the physical systems around us deform, move, and carry load. It explains, for example, why a helmet liner cushions an impact, why a rubber band stiffens near its limit, and why a plastic film tears easily in one direction. In my research, I seek the mechanisms that govern how polymeric materials and structures deform and use those mechanisms to design resilient and sustainable engineered systems. I hope to pass on to students the curiosity behind this work: to look at a physical system and ask how it works and which mechanism governs it. I also want them to leave my courses with a way of approaching and solving the problems that interest them, including those that matter for a resilient and sustainable future.

In the classroom, I start from physical intuition and then develop the formalism, derive on the board, pair each abstraction with a drawing, and use computation as a way of thinking that is checked against theory and experiment.
